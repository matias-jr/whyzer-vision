Workflow and narrative

Two different jobs

Rox and Whyzer both show up when you search for AI account research. They were built to answer different questions.

Rox asks

How do we get more done across every account?

Rox is an AI agent platform for revenue teams. Its agents research accounts, enrich contacts, monitor for changes, run multi-channel outbound, write meeting briefs, and sync it all to the CRM. Rox describes itself as revenue agents for the Global 2000. It's built to take work off a rep's plate and keep the pipeline moving.

Whyzer asks

What do I say to this CFO that earns the meeting?

Whyzer is an account intelligence tool for the individual enterprise seller. It reads the company's financial story (10-Ks, earnings calls, executive podcasts) and turns it into a sourced point of view, persona-tailored emails, and executive decks. It's built to make one conversation land.

Most enterprise sellers don't lose deals because they ran out of time to research. They lose them because the first email sounded like every other first email, and the executive never answered.

Where Rox is stronger

Rox is a serious product, and for a lot of buyers it's the better fit.

Breadth

Research, contact discovery, outbound, meeting prep, and CRM hygiene live in one system. If your current stack is five tools stitched together, Rox can replace several of them.

It keeps working when you don't

Rox agents monitor accounts continuously and surface changes. Whyzer doesn't monitor in the background.

Built for teams

The Teams plan includes unlimited seats, shared pipeline, CRM sync and writeback, and user management. Enterprise adds data warehouse sync, SSO, and a dedicated deployment team.

Enterprise credibility

Rox counts MongoDB, Cloud Software Group, and Together AI among its customers. It was named a Gartner Cool Vendor in AI for B2B Sales Productivity in 2025 and a Major Player in IDC's 2026 MarketScape for unified revenue orchestration.

It's easy to start

Rox now offers a free start and an Individual plan. You don't need a sales cycle to try it.

If your problem is a fragmented sales stack across 50 reps, Rox is solving a problem Whyzer doesn't touch.

Stacking

Can you use both?

Yes, and plenty of teams will. A team on Rox for pipeline workflow can still have its strategic sellers use Whyzer for the executive point of view on their top accounts. Rox handles pipeline workflow across the team, and Whyzer sharpens the handful of executive conversations that decide the year.

If you're an individual AE, the question is simpler. What's costing you more right now: hours of manual workflow, or executives who don't reply?

FAQ

About Rox

What is Rox AI?+

Rox is an AI agent platform for revenue teams. Its agents research and monitor accounts, enrich contacts, run outbound, prepare meeting briefs, and sync to CRMs like Salesforce and HubSpot.

How much does Rox cost?+

As of October 2026, Rox is free to start. The Individual plan is $100/month for 10,000 agent actions, Teams is $255/month for 15,000 actions with unlimited seats, and Enterprise pricing is on request. Check rox.com/pricing for current details.

Is Rox good for individual sellers?+

Rox has an Individual plan built for reps who want research, monitoring, and contact discovery across their accounts. If what you need most is an executive-ready financial argument on a specific account, a tool built for that job, like Whyzer, is a closer fit.

Does Rox analyze SEC filings and earnings calls?+

Rox's public materials focus on account research, enrichment, and workflow automation. We couldn't confirm dedicated 10-K, earnings call, or proxy analysis. Test it on a public account during the free start.

What's a good Rox alternative for individual enterprise AEs?+

If you want a self-serve tool focused on the executive conversation, Whyzer reads a company's filings and earnings calls and turns them into a sourced point of view, for $57/month. Salesmotion is another self-serve option focused on signals and contacts. See the full comparison of AI account intelligence tools.
