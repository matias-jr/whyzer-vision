The problem

Every seller has AI research now. Executives still aren't replying.

Open your inbox and count the "personalized" emails you got this week. Each one mentions something real about your company. A funding round. A new hire. A line from a press release.

None of them made you reply.

Your executives are getting the same emails. More of them every quarter. AI made research cheap, so every seller now shows up with the same facts.

What's still scarce is the argument.

What the numbers say about where this company is under pressure. Why that pressure lands on this specific executive. Why it has to be fixed this year.

Every tool on this list can find you a news article. Far fewer can tell you what to say about it to a CFO. So we compared nine tools against the job that matters most to an enterprise seller: getting a senior executive to take the meeting, and walking in with something worth their time.

Method

How we evaluated these tools

Eight criteria. Each one maps to a moment in a real enterprise deal.

01

Financial depth

Does it read what the company tells investors: 10-Ks, earnings calls, proxy statements? A press release tells you what a company wants you to think. An earnings call tells you what analysts are pushing on.

02

Output type

Data, signals, or a finished point of view. Data still needs you to do the thinking. A point of view is something you can put in front of an executive.

03

Verifiability

Can you trace every claim to its source before you say it out loud? The fastest way to lose a CFO is a number you can't defend.

04

Coverage

Public companies are the easy part. Can the tool help on private companies and accounts outside the US?

05

Buying model

Can an individual seller sign up and use it today, or does it need a procurement cycle and a budget owner?

06

Pricing model

Flat, per seat, per action, per account, or enterprise contract. The model matters as much as the number once you're working a full territory.

07

Workflow

CRM integration, always-on monitoring, team collaboration. These matter a lot for teams. Less for a seller trying to crack one account this week.

08

Methodology

Is there a selling framework behind the output? AI can write. It can't tell you which argument wins a seven-figure deal unless someone who has won them built that in.

PG:AI

FAQ

Questions sellers ask

What is an AI account research tool?+

Software that gathers information about a target company (financial filings, earnings calls, news, hiring, leadership changes) and uses AI to summarize it. The better tools go further and turn that research into something you can use with a buyer: a point of view, an email, a meeting brief.

Is there a cheaper alternative to Databook?+

Databook is sold as an enterprise contract and doesn't publish pricing. Self-serve tools that also read financial filings include Whyzer ($57/month), Salesmotion ($85/month), and Rox ($100/month for Individual). They differ in what they hand you: Whyzer focuses on the executive point of view, Salesmotion on signals and contacts, Rox on agent-driven workflow.

Can ChatGPT do account research for enterprise sales?+

It can summarize documents you find and give it. It doesn't source its claims by default, it can state wrong numbers confidently, and it has no selling methodology. For a first look at an account, it works. For something you'll say to a CFO, verify every number first.

What's the difference between sales intelligence and a point of view tool?+

Sales intelligence tells you who to contact and what changed: contacts, firmographics, signals. A point of view tool tells you what it means for that company and what to say about it to a specific executive. Most enterprise sellers need both. Most have plenty of the first.

Which tools work for private or non-US companies?+

Coverage varies and changes often, so test your own accounts during a trial. Whyzer covers 10,000+ companies, including 3,527 private companies, across 150+ markets.

Do I need procurement approval to buy one?+

Not always. Whyzer, Rox (Individual), Salesmotion (Individual), and PG:AI can be started self-serve. Databook and the larger team platforms are typically bought through a company sales process.

The executive meeting doesn't go to the seller with the most research. It goes to the seller with the best argument.
