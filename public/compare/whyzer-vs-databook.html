Credit where due

Databook set the standard

Give Databook its due.

It proved that financial intelligence belongs in enterprise selling. That a seller who understands a customer's numbers earns a different kind of meeting than a seller who leads with product. Its customers include Microsoft, Databricks, Salesforce, and Schneider Electric. It's a G2 Leader in Market Intelligence (Summer 2026), and its customers report results like a 4x lift in executive engagement.

Databook today calls itself a GTM decision system. It joins licensed, proprietary, human-verified third-party data with your CRM, call, and planning data to build each account's case for change. Then it pushes that case into the tools your teams already use, with agentic workflows across sales, marketing, value engineering, and customer success.

The catch is access.

Databook is sold to the organization. It starts with a 90-day proof of value and is built for enterprise revenue teams ready to commit to a transformation program. Most enterprise sellers will never have a login.

Where Databook is stronger

Data depth and verification

Databook licenses third-party data and has it human-verified, with source, date, and provenance on every fact. That's a level of data operations an individual tool doesn't replicate.

It joins outside data with inside data

Databook connects account intelligence with your CRM notes, deal activity, and relationship context, so the whole team works from one view of the account.

Delivered where your team works

CRM, Slack, Teams, your own front end, or via API and MCP. No new destination app.

Services, not just software

Forward-deployed engineers, AI strategists, and value architects embed with your team. Its AI bootcamps train sellers on their live deals.

Enterprise credibility

Analyst recognition, a long list of Fortune 500 customers, and a commercial model tied to outcomes.

If your company is ready to roll account intelligence out across hundreds of sellers and wants it wired into every system, Databook is built for that.

FAQ

About Databook

How much does Databook cost?+

Databook doesn't publish pricing. As of October 2026, it sells through an enterprise process that starts with a 90-day proof of value, with an outcome-based commercial model.

Can individual sellers buy Databook?+

Databook is sold to organizations rather than to individual sellers. If your company hasn't bought it, a self-serve tool like Whyzer is the practical option.

Is there a cheaper alternative to Databook?+

For individual sellers, Whyzer offers financially grounded executive POVs at $57/month, self-serve. It doesn't replicate Databook's licensed data operation, CRM integration, or team-wide workflows. It's built for the seller preparing for an executive conversation.

Does Databook cover private or non-US companies?+

Check with Databook for current coverage. Whyzer covers 10,000+ companies, including 3,527 private companies, across 150+ markets.

How does Whyzer's financial data compare to Databook's?+

Databook licenses third-party data and has it human-verified, then joins it with your internal systems. Whyzer reads public financial disclosures (10-Ks, earnings calls, executive podcasts) and links every claim in its output to the source. Databook goes deeper on data operations.
