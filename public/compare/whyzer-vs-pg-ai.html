The core difference

Same promise, opposite starting points

If you've found both of these tools, you're probably shopping for the same thing: a point of view on any account, without spending a weekend in a 10-K.

Both tools promise that. Both use the same language. Points of view. Value hypotheses. Executive messaging. The difference is where each one starts building.

Seller-out

PG:AI starts with you.

It's trained on your company's solutions, use cases, and messaging. It analyzes the account from thousands of public sources, including earnings calls, then maps your value proposition onto what it finds. The output answers a clear question: where does our product fit this account?

Account-in

Whyzer starts with them.

It reads the account's financial story first. What the filings say. What executives told analysts on the last earnings call. Where margins are tightening, where capital is moving, what leadership is on record promising to fix. The point of view is built from that pressure outward.

To be fair to PG:AI: it analyzes the account's priorities too. The contrast here is starting point and emphasis. Both tools look at the account.

Why the starting point matters: executives respond to their own problems, described in their own numbers. Your value proposition becomes relevant once they've agreed the problem is real.

One question asks where your product fits this account. The other asks what this company's CFO is under pressure to fix. Answer the second one well, and the first usually answers itself.

Where PG:AI is stronger

PG:AI is a well-built product, and for the right buyer it's the better choice.

Team rollout

PG:AI is designed for GTM teams. If you need twenty reps producing account research that sounds like one company, it's built for that.

Trained on your messaging

Because PG:AI learns your solutions and use cases, the output stays on-brand and maps directly to what your company sells. Enablement leaders love this, and they should.

Account monitoring

You can subscribe to account updates and keep a running view of what's changing. Whyzer doesn't monitor accounts in the background.

More content types

Beyond the point of view, PG:AI generates emails, call scripts, notes, and documents through its AI assistant.

Named customers in enterprise tech

Its customer list includes companies like Coralogix and Apptio.

If you're an enablement leader standardizing account research across a team, PG:AI is built for that job. Whyzer isn't.

The executive conversation

Where Whyzer is stronger

The financial story comes first

A Whyzer POV starts where the executive's attention already is: their numbers, their pressures, the commitments they've made to the board and the street. That's what makes an executive stop and read.

A method from someone who sold this way

The structure behind every Whyzer POV comes from Jamal Reimer, who closed $160M+ in enterprise deals as an individual contributor. Observation, implication, reframe, approach, future state. It's the sequence he used to get executives to take meetings, built into the software.

Coaching when the deal gets hard

Elite members get the Vault content library and live deal coaching. A POV gets you the meeting. Winning the deal takes judgment, and judgment comes from people who've been there.

Verifiable, line by line

Every claim in a Whyzer POV links to its source. In front of a CFO, that's the difference between authority and getting caught out.

FAQ

About PG:AI

What is PG:AI?+

PG:AI is an AI account intelligence platform for GTM teams. It analyzes public sources on each account, generates strategic insights, points of view, and stakeholder content, and maps your organization's value proposition to each account's needs.

How much does PG:AI cost?+

As of October 2026, PG:AI prices on accounts and AI credits, with a 30-day free start. See getpg.ai/pricing for current plan prices.

How do PG:AI credits work?+

PG:AI meters usage on two dimensions: the number of accounts you work and the AI credits you use. Check PG:AI's pricing page for how each plan allocates them.

Does PG:AI use SEC filings and earnings calls?+

PG:AI says it collects and analyzes data from thousands of public sources, and its documentation lists earnings call analysis among its features. Test a public account during the free start to see how deep it goes on filings.

What's the difference between PG:AI and Whyzer?+

PG:AI starts from your value proposition and maps it to the account, which makes it strong for team rollouts. Whyzer starts from the account's financial story, builds the executive argument from there using Jamal Reimer's selling method, and adds live coaching on Elite.

Is PG:AI good for individual sellers?+

It can be used by individuals, and it's designed with GTM teams in mind. If you're an individual seller whose priority is executive engagement on strategic accounts, compare it against Whyzer on one of your own accounts. See the full comparison of AI account intelligence tools.
